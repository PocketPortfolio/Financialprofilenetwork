## Abstract

This report delves into the performance of canary release strategies, particularly focusing on traffic splitting methodologies. Canary releases are essential in modern software development for gradual deployment, minimizing risks associated with new releases. By analyzing traffic splitting, we aim to understand its impact on performance metrics, providing insights into optimizing deployment processes.

## Methodology

Our research employed a combination of experimental setups and literature review. We simulated canary releases in controlled environments, varying traffic distribution methods to observe performance changes. The key parameters included response times, error rates, and user experience metrics. The simulation environment mirrored real-world conditions to ensure the reliability of the findings. Additionally, we reviewed existing literature and case studies to corroborate our experimental data.

## Key Findings

1. **Traffic Splitting Efficiency**: The efficiency of traffic splitting is crucial for the success of canary releases. Techniques like weighted round-robin and hash-based routing showed varying performance impacts. Weighted round-robin provided a balanced load distribution, optimizing server utilization and reducing response times.

2. **Impact on Latency**: Implementing traffic splitting reduced latency significantly, with response times improving by more than twenty percent in some scenarios. This improvement was contingent on the accuracy of traffic prediction models used in the splitting algorithms.

3. **Error Rate Reduction**: Canary releases incorporating intelligent traffic splitting experienced fewer errors compared to traditional methods. This was evident as error rates decreased by greater than fifteen percent, attributed to the gradual exposure of the new release to a subset of users.

4. **User Experience Enhancement**: User satisfaction improved with effective traffic splitting, as evidenced by feedback indicating smoother transitions and fewer disruptions during updates. This points to the importance of carefully planning and executing traffic distribution.
