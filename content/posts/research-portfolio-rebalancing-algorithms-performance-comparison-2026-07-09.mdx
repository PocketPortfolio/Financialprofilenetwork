## Abstract

In an ever-evolving financial market, portfolio rebalancing is a critical strategy to maintain desired asset allocation, optimize returns, and manage risks. This research report delves into various portfolio rebalancing algorithms, evaluating their performance through comparative analysis. By exploring methodologies such as calendar, threshold, and volatility-based rebalancing, this study aims to provide valuable insights into selecting the most effective algorithm for different investment scenarios. As investors seek strategies that align with their risk tolerance and financial goals, understanding the nuances of these algorithms becomes crucial.

## Methodology

The analysis began by selecting a diverse range of rebalancing algorithms known for their unique approaches and potential benefits. These included:

1. **Calendar Rebalancing**: Regular time-based rebalancing, typically monthly or quarterly.
2. **Threshold Rebalancing**: Trigger-based rebalancing when asset allocations deviate by a pre-set percentage from the target.
3. **Volatility-Based Rebalancing**: Adjustments made based on market volatility, aiming to capitalize on market fluctuations.

Each algorithm was implemented using historical market data across various asset classes, including equities, bonds, and cryptocurrencies. Performance metrics such as return on investment (ROI), Sharpe ratio, and drawdown rates were measured to assess the efficiency and robustness of each strategy. The JSON-based Investment Tracker was utilized to systematically collect and analyze data, ensuring a rigorous and consistent approach to performance evaluation.

## Key Findings

The comparative analysis revealed several key insights into the performance of portfolio rebalancing algorithms:

- **Calendar Rebalancing**: This algorithm showed consistent performance with moderate ROI and reduced transaction costs due to its predictable nature. However, it was less responsive to sudden market changes, resulting in suboptimal performance during periods of high volatility.

- **Threshold Rebalancing**: Exhibited superior performance in dynamically adjusting portfolios to align with market conditions. This approach achieved higher ROI and improved risk management, particularly during volatile market conditions, but at the cost of increased transaction frequency and associated costs.

- **Volatility-Based Rebalancing**: Provided the most adaptive strategy, effectively balancing risk and reward by leveraging market volatility. This method yielded the highest Sharpe ratios, indicating better risk-adjusted returns, yet required complex calculations and continuous market monitoring.

The study concluded that no single algorithm universally outperforms others; instead, the choice depends on specific investor goals, risk appetite, and market conditions.
